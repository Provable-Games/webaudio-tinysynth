## 2. Contract as implemented

**Fields** (on an operator; all optional, absent means no filter):

| Field | Meaning | Accepted values |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `fl` | filter type | `"lowpass"`, `"highpass"` or `"bandpass"` |
| `ff` | cutoff (low- and high-pass) or centre (band-pass) | a normal positive 32-bit float: a number from 2^-126 (1.1754943508222875e-38) whose float32 is finite; Hz with `fk` 0, a multiple of the note-on frequency with `fk` 1. Required with `fl` |
| `fq` | conventional linear Q | a normal positive 32-bit float, as `ff`; default `Math.SQRT1_2` (Butterworth for low- and high-pass) |
| `fk` | key tracking | `0` or `1`; default `0` |

An `undefined` field counts as absent, as `setTimbre`'s defaults do. These are D-028's natural limits: the type, the operator being an output, normal positive 32-bit floats. That has two parts.

**The floor 2^-126 (review F1).** Low- and high-pass `fq` below the smallest normal float32 give NaN coefficients: `α = sin ω0 / (2·10^(Q/20))` overflows once `10^(Q/20)` underflows. In Chromium the boundary is exactly 2^-126; in Firefox and WebKit it is about 1e-308. One such note turns the whole output into NaN, silencing every channel; in Firefox the silence lasts until the context is replaced (T12-review F1, `_evidence/t12-review/`). This branch's probe (`scratch/floor-probe.js`, `results/floor-prefix-*.json` before the fix, `floor-fixed-*.json` after) reproduces the Chromium NaN at `fq` 1e-39 and 1e-300. At 2^-126 exactly, 45 settings per engine render finite and leave a later note unchanged.

The same floor applies to `ff`. No accepted `ff` gave NaN or silenced another channel in any engine: 51 settings per engine down to 5e-324, with the default, the smallest and the largest `fq`. But below the floor the cutoff the node stores is not a normal positive frequency: it reads back exactly 0 Hz below about 7e-46 and a subnormal value up to 2^-126, in all three engines. That is outside D-028's "(0, Nyquist)". One rule covers both fields and costs 7 bytes. `fk` 1 can still produce a subnormal cutoff under extreme negative tuning, which renders without NaN.

At the top, the other part is "finite as a 32-bit float": `AudioParam.value` is a WebIDL `float`, and assigning a value outside the float range throws a `TypeError` in all three engines (`results/nyquist-probe.json`, `throws`: `Q.value = 1e39` throws, `3.4e38` does not). Band-pass assigns `fq` unchanged, so `fq` 1e39 would otherwise pass `setTimbre` and throw inside `_note`, after the voice's first nodes exist. The same rule is applied to `ff` for one uniform check, although the clamp keeps every computed cutoff in range.

**Routing.** An operator with `fl` that outputs audio (`g` 0) routes oscillator (or buffer source) → its gain `g[i]` → `BiquadFilterNode` → the channel volume node, in place of gain → channel volume. Without `fl` nothing changes: no node is created, the same calls are made in the same order, so the upstream differential still passes unchanged. In `_note` the change is the gain's output connection, `g[i].connect(pn.g==0 && pn.fl ? (q[i]=this._filter(pn,f,out)) : out)`, a `const q=[]` per voice, and `q` in the two voice records. `pn.g==0` guards timbres written past `setTimbre` (TinyChip writes program slots directly, D-027): filter fields on a modulator are ignored there, so an FM or AM path is never filtered.

**`_filter(pn, f, out)`** creates the node, sets `type = fl`, `frequency = min(fk ? f·ff : ff, 0.45 × sampleRate)` and `Q = fq` for band-pass or `20·log10(fq)` dB for low- and high-pass, connects it to `out` and returns it. The frequency and Q are set once with `.value`: the filter is fixed for the life of the note.

**Key-tracking basis.** `f` is the frequency computed at the top of `_note`: `440·2^((n − 69 + masterTuningC + tuningC[ch] + masterTuningF + tuningF[ch]/8192 + scaleTuning[ch][n mod 12]) / 12)`, so master, channel and scale tuning are included. Operator ratio `t` and offset `f`, bend, the pitch envelope and modulation are excluded (unit-tested). For drums `f` derives from the drum note number; fixed Hz is the normal choice there.

**Q conversion** (Web Audio 1.0 §BiquadFilterNode, D-007): low- and high-pass use `α = sin ω0 / (2·10^(Q/20))`, so the engine is given `20·log10(fq)` dB; band-pass uses `α = sin ω0 / (2Q)` and is given `fq`. Both are checked against an independent RBJ computation in every engine (§4).

### Nyquist margin: 0.45 × sample rate

The engine clamps the computed cutoff to `0.45 × sampleRate` (19,845 Hz at 44.1 kHz, 21,600 Hz at 48 kHz) and does not rely on browser clamping. The lower end needs no clamp: validation makes every computed cutoff > 0 (a value below the float range rounds to 0 in the AudioParam, which every engine handles without NaN).

Evidence (`scratch/nyquist-probe.js`, `results/nyquist-probe.json`): in each engine, low-, high- and band-pass at cutoff fractions r = f0/SR from 0.40 to 0.6, Q 0.1, 1/√2, 1, 10 and 30, at 44.1 and 48 kHz. `getFrequencyResponse` was compared on 512 log-spaced frequencies with a double-precision RBJ magnitude, and a one-second impulse render with the RBJ difference equation.

| r | Max response difference, all engines (dB) | Max impulse-response difference | −80 dB settle, low-pass Q 1/√2 (samples) | Low-pass Q 30 | High-pass Q 30 | Band-pass Q 30 |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 0.40 | < 1e-4 | 7.1e-6 | 21 | 888 | 943 | 895–945 |
| **0.45** | < 1e-4 | 1.5e-5 | 33 | 1,565 | 1,786 | 1,279–1,791 |
| 0.48 | < 1e-4 | 1.9e-5 | 80 | 3,413 | 4,219–4,414 | 2,559–4,402 |
| 0.49 | < 1e-4 | 2.7e-5 | 151 | 6,129 | 7,423–8,781 | 4,223–8,802 |
| 0.499 | 0.0002 | 2.9e-5 | 907 | 39,286 | > 44,099 | 18,559–> 44,099 |
| 0.4999 | 0.0028 | 5.5e-5 | 2,681–2,819 | > 44,099 | > 44,099 | > 44,099 |

(Settle times at 44.1 kHz; ranges span the engines; "> 44,099" means not settled within the one-second render.)

- **Accuracy does not set the margin.** All three engines track RBJ to within 0.003 dB right up to 0.4999 × SR, so there is no accuracy cliff below Nyquist.
- **At and above Nyquist the response collapses, and each engine reports it differently.** At r ≥ 0.5 every engine renders low-pass as identity, and high-pass and band-pass as silence (impulse peak 1 or 0). With Q > 1/√2 that is a discontinuity: at 0.4999 a resonant low-pass still has its Q-high peak, and at 0.5 it has none. Reading back a 30 kHz `frequency.value` gives 22,050 in Chromium and WebKit and 30,000 in Firefox. The implicit clamping differs observably, so the engine must not rely on it.
- **Ring time sets the margin.** As r → 0.5 the poles approach z = −1 and the ring time grows as 1/sin(2πr), without bound. The pole radius depends only on sin(2πr)/(2Q), so at 0.45 the clamp decays at the same rate as a cutoff at 0.05 × SR (2.2/2.4 kHz) with the same Q: 33 samples to −80 dB at Q 1/√2 and 1,565 (35 ms) at Q 30. At 0.49 it is 6,129 samples (139 ms) at Q 30, and at 0.499 0.9 s. A voice's routes are disconnected when its sources end, so a long ring past that point would be cut off.
- **Audibility (RBJ, Q 1/√2).** At the 0.45 clamp, a low-pass is down 0.34 dB at 18 kHz and 3.7 dB at 20 kHz (44.1 kHz), or 0.09 and 0.50 dB (48 kHz). A key-tracked low-pass whose computed cutoff passes Nyquist therefore stays open to within 0.34 dB up to 18 kHz. A high-pass clamped there passes white noise below 20 kHz at −15.7 dB (44.1 kHz) or −22.5 dB (48 kHz) of its unfiltered total energy: near-silence, not the exact silence a cutoff at Nyquist gives. 0.40 would cost 3.9 dB at 18 kHz; 0.48 would lengthen the ring 2.2× for 0.3 dB at 18 kHz.

So 0.45 × SR, the starting point D-007 suggested, stands: the measurements did not justify a different constant. Tested at 44.1 and 48 kHz, fixed and key-tracked, in the unit tests (parameter values) and in rendered audio (high-pass `fk` 1 `ff` 8 on notes 100–108, §4).

### Validation: `_checkFilter(o)` (for T5)

`setTimbre` calls it with one line, first, before any change: `for(let i=0;i<p.length;) this._checkFilter(p[i++]);`. The call sits at the top of `setTimbre`, and the helper sits before `setTimbre`, so T11's wave check after `filldef` merges without conflict (§3).

Contract, for one operator `o`. It throws, and changes nothing (no slot, no caller object), in this order:
1. `fl` absent: `TypeError("ff without fl")` (or `fq`, `fk`) for the first of `ff`, `fq`, `fk` given; otherwise return.
2. `fl` not one of the three strings: `TypeError("fl: " + String(fl))`.
3. `g` given and `g != 0` (the engine's own loose test, so `"0"` counts as an output): `TypeError("fl on a modulator")`.
4. `ff`, always, then `fq` if given: not a number → `TypeError("ff: …")`; not > 0 or not finite as a float → `RangeError("ff: …")`.
5. `fk` if given: not a number → `TypeError`; not 0 or 1 → `RangeError("fk: …")`.

It reads nothing but the operator, and nothing is filled in: no `fq` or `fk` default is stored, so the editor round trip `setTimbre(m, n, synth.program[n].p)` reinstalls a filtered timbre (tested). T5 folds this into its per-operator validator with the same behavior. With T5's copy-and-normalize, it should still not add `fq`/`fk` to timbres without `fl`. Every built-in timbre passes in both qualities.

### Lifecycle

The filter belongs to the voice: `q[i]` sits beside `o[i]` and `g[i]` in the melodic (`notetab`) and percussion (`_src`) records, and is disconnected wherever the voice's routes are released:
- `_pruneNote` (melodic release and timer prune, voice stealing, all-sound-off, the D-023 caller stop of melodic voices and of percussion hits sounding or queued ahead): from the source's `ended` handler, with the source and gain, as T4 does (the Chromium never-ending-oscillator case);
- the percussion `ended` handler in `_note` (a hit that ends by itself);
- `_drop` (context replacement and `dispose()`): at once on a context the synth closes, or that is closed or offline; otherwise from `ended`.

**Failed allocation (review F3).** On validated filter fields nothing in the filter path can throw: the clamp keeps the frequency in range, and the float rules keep Q in range and finite. The filter is created inside the operator loop. On this branch alone, a note whose allocation fails part-way can still orphan a filter, as it orphans the operator's source and gain. That class predates T12: the base `d1f0e26` orphans the source and gain the same way (T12-review F3, mock and Chromium). Examples are an unknown wave (`setPeriodicWave` throws), a route to a missing operator (`g:15` on operator 1), or a non-finite computed value. The filter adds one node to it.

**Integration conditions** that close it. Each must reject before the operator loop, so that no source, gain or filter exists:
1. **T11's wave check:** every operator's wave is resolved before `_limitVoices` and before any node.
2. **T5's route validation:** `g` must name an earlier operator (D-010 Q12).
3. **T5.2's guard** for non-finite computed values (large `k`, long FM chains; D-030).

After T5 integrates, a regression test should assert that a note rejected for any of these reasons creates no source, gain or filter.

## 4. Response evidence and tolerances

All runs: `tests/browser/specs/filters.js`, both builds, 44.1 and 48 kHz. Expected values come from the contract and a double-precision RBJ biquad in Node (`rbj`, `mag` in the spec), not from the library.

**`getFrequencyResponse` of each voice's filter node.** 122 filters per sample rate: three types × cutoffs 250, 1,000, 5,000 and 15,000 Hz × Q default, 0.1, 1, 4 and 30; key tracking with `ff` 1, 2 and 8 at notes 24, 48, 69, 96, 108 and 120 (the clamp included); `ff` 30,000 Hz and `ff` = Nyquist; two drums. Each is evaluated at 256 log-spaced frequencies from 20 Hz to 0.999 × Nyquist; points where RBJ is below −80 dB are skipped. Measured worst difference: 1.0e-4 dB (44.1 kHz) and 1.1e-4 dB (48 kHz), identical in all three engines. The figure holds for this grid. On a 200-point grid the reviewer measured 4.06e-4 dB, for band-pass at the top of the band, at float32 resolution (review F6). Tolerance `RESPONSE_DB` = 0.01 dB.

**Rendered tone probes.** OfflineAudioContext, reverb off, masterVol 0.05, so the compressor stays below its threshold, as in T6's render spec. The compressor's fixed makeup gain and 6 ms delay are the same in both renders and cancel in the ratio.
- Input: one sine operator held at frequency F (sustain 1) in its own 0.6 s slot.
- Measurement: the tone's amplitude, by a least-squares sin/cos fit at F over 0.2–0.45 s after the note-on.
- Normalization: the same render without the filter fields.
- Probes (24 per sample rate): low-pass 1 kHz at 250, 1,000 and 4,000 Hz; high-pass 1 kHz `fq` 4 (12.04 dB) at the same three; band-pass 1 kHz `fq` 4 (linear) at 500, 1,000 and 2,000 Hz; low-pass 1 kHz `fq` 10 at the cutoff (+20 dB); key tracking (low-pass `fk` 1 `ff` 1 at notes 36–105: −3.01 dB at each); the clamp (high-pass `fk` 1 `ff` 8 at notes 84–108, notes 100–108 clamped at both rates); two drums.
- Worst |measured − RBJ| over both builds and rates: 1.6e-5 dB (Chromium), 3.5e-5 dB (Firefox), 1.1e-5 dB (WebKit). Tolerance `PROBE_DB` = 0.02 dB.

The clamp probes are sharp: near Nyquist the bilinear warping makes the clamped high-pass steep, so at note 108 RBJ expects −52.5 dB at 44.1 kHz. A mutant clamping at 0.49 × SR measures −80.6 dB there (28 dB off), and one without the clamp renders silence (−∞ dB; the engines output nothing for a cutoff at or above Nyquist) (§7).

**Consumer fixture, band energies.** The fixture's three timbres (§6) are rendered with and without their filters, 2 s slots. Per octave band from 125 Hz to 16 kHz (Blackman-Harris window, 16,384 samples, 4,096 for the 0.14 s hi-hat, from 10 ms after each note-on), the filtered render's energy relative to the unfiltered render's must equal that of the unfiltered render passed through the independent RBJ filter of the declared setting, with the same window. The chain after the filter is linear, so the filter commutes with it.
- 24 bands per sample rate are above −60 dB. Worst difference: 0.086 dB (Chromium), 0.051 dB (Firefox) and 0.021 dB (WebKit). Tolerance `BAND_DB` = 0.5 dB.
- Bands expected below −60 dB must measure at least 50 dB down. None arose with this fixture.

A first version that weighted the unfiltered spectrum by |H|² per bin missed by up to 2.7 dB in steep-slope bands. That is window leakage across the band edge, which only a time-domain filter models; the spec now uses one.

The voice lists (`notetab`, `_src`, `_gone`) hold no filter afterwards.

**Browser (instrumented realtime graph, T6's `page/instrument.js`, both builds, all three engines).**
- Three phases on a synth-created context, each followed by a bounded wait until every voice is pruned and every source but the LFO has ended:
  - 60 melodic notes with steals (`voices` 6) and 20 drum hits;
  - a song with filtered programs and drums, stopped by `stopMIDI()` with two filtered hits queued ahead;
  - held notes, then all-sound-off (CC 120).
- After each phase, every node-to-node connection is exactly the idle graph's. A filter has only node-to-node connections (gain → filter → channel volume), so none from or to a filter is left. The voice lists hold no filter.

- Program 1 filters both of its outputs, and drum 38 only its second (operator 1), so every release path is exercised on later operators too (review F2).
- Then replacement: the synth's context, with filtered voices playing, has no live connection right after `setAudioContext()`.
- Then `dispose()` on the caller's new context: it stays open, and once its sources end it has no live connection and the synth holds no filter.

**Finding (pre-existing, not T12; for T4/T6).** In WebKit only, the instrumentation keeps some `chmod → source.detune` keys after voices end: 6 to 28 per run, varying (17 to 28 in the final matrix; 0 in Chromium and Firefox). ## 8. Cost and size

**Graph cost.** A timbre without `fl` creates no node and makes no extra call; the differential pins this. A filtered output operator adds one `BiquadFilterNode` and one connection per voice. It is released with the voice: with the sources' end, or at once on teardown (§5). So node count and live connections grow linearly with filtered polyphony, are bounded by `voices` and drum hits, and return to the idle graph. The unit tests count exactly one filter per filtered operator per note (300 notes: 300 filters plus the one warm-up note on a filtered program 0). The browser lifecycle check shows the return to the idle graph in all three engines.

## 9. Risks and notes

1. **Size.** See §8. The validator's descriptive messages are most of it (D-013 style); gzip compresses their repeats.
2. **Timbres written past `setTimbre`** (TinyChip's program slots) are not validated. Filter fields on a modulator are ignored there, never applied. A non-finite `ff` or `fq` written that way would throw in `_note`, which is the T5.2 class of non-finite computed values.
3. **Failed allocation** (review F3, pre-existing): closed at integration by T11's wave check, T5's route validation and T5.2's guard, each before the operator loop (§2).
4. **High-pass at the clamp is near-silent, not silent** (§2): a key-tracked high-pass whose computed cutoff passes Nyquist keeps a 0.45–0.5 × SR band.
5. **Filter tail.** A filter is disconnected when its voice's sources end (3.5·r after release, 3.5·d[0] for drums), cutting any remaining ring. At the 0.45 clamp the worst case is a Q 30 ring of 35 ms. Low cutoffs with high Q ring longer: 6,129 samples (0.14 s) to −80 dB at 441 Hz, Q 30, the same decay as 0.49 × SR. That is the same voice-lifetime rule D-010 Q8 documents for operators. Two extreme accepted settings show it more strongly, without NaN (`results/floor-fixed-*.json`). A low- or high-pass `fq` at the float maximum is a lossless resonator, so its ring lasts as long as the voice; a later note in an offline render, where voices are not pruned, measured 1.41× louder. A sub-hertz high-pass leaves a slowly decaying DC offset after the note's gate (1.16× in Chromium and WebKit at 0.001 Hz). In realtime both end when the voice is pruned and its filter disconnected.
6. **WebKit detune-key residue** (§5): pre-existing, reported for T4/T6.

## 10. Draft README/NOTICE text (for the supervisor, CONCURRENCY rule 4)

README, "What is added" (append):
> Optional fixed filters on an operator's output: the timbre fields `fl`, `ff`, `fq` and `fk` (see [Timbre Object Structure](#timbre-object-structure)). A timbre without `fl` builds exactly the same graph as before.

README, "What behaves differently" (new bullet):
> - `setTimbre()` checks the filter fields before changing anything and throws a `TypeError` or `RangeError` for a filter field without `fl`, an unknown `fl`, a filter on a modulator (`g` ≠ 0), or an `ff`, `fq` or `fk` out of range. Timbres without filter fields are accepted as before.

README, "Timbre Object Structure", after `k`:
> * fl: optional fixed filter on this operator's output, `"lowpass"`, `"highpass"` or `"bandpass"`. Only on an operator with `g:0`. Without `fl` there is no filter.
> * ff: cutoff (low- and high-pass) or centre (band-pass) frequency, a normal positive 32-bit float (at least 2^-126): Hz, or with `fk:1` a multiple of the note's frequency (including master, channel and scale tuning; not the operator's `t` or `f`, bend or pitch envelope). Required with `fl`. Clamped to 0.45 × the sample rate.
> * fq: resonance as a linear Q, a normal positive 32-bit float (at least 2^-126), default 0.7071 (`Math.SQRT1_2`). Low- and high-pass are given `20·log10(fq)` dB, band-pass `fq`.
> * fk: 1 to track the note frequency, 0 (default) for fixed Hz.
>
> The filter is fixed for the life of the note, and is released with the voice. Example, a hi-hat through a 3 kHz high-pass: `[{w:"n1",t:0,f:440,v:0.3,d:0.04,fl:"highpass",ff:3000}]`.

README, `setTimbre(m,n,p)` entry (append):
> Throws a `TypeError` or `RangeError` for invalid filter fields (see [Timbre Object Structure](#timbre-object-structure)); nothing changes then.

NOTICE (new bullet):
>   - webaudio-tinysynth.js: added optional fixed operator filters (timbre fields fl, ff, fq and fk): a BiquadFilterNode between an output operator's gain and its channel, key-trackable and clamped below Nyquist, released with its voice. setTimbre() rejects invalid filter fields before any change.
