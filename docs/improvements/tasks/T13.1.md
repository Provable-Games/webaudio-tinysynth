## 1. The failure

CI log (`ci-job.log`, line 3209): `FAIL short-notes held min: until the note-off the short note renders as the uncut note (max |diff| <= 0.0002 of the peak) (q0 126 @0.0700 s: 1.47e+0)`.

- One program (126, Applause, quality 0), one duration, one build. The other renders of that case, and the same case on `source`, were within 1.7e-5.
- 1.47 of the peak is not a tolerance miss. It is a whole segment of one render that differs from an otherwise identical render.
- PR #60 does not touch the engine or the spec, and the case passed on re-run.

Reproduced locally with the unmodified spec (WebKit, `--engines=webkit --specs=short-notes`, 12 runs, `logs/base-*.log`): run 2 failed `held source` (q0 program 127, 1.83 of the peak), and the `completed` case reconciled a glitch in runs 4 (q0 126, source) and 10 (q0 122, min). The programs, durations and builds differ between events.

## 2. Policy as built (#78)

**The first attempt decides.** A note that is missing or too quiet, a large finite transient, a non-finite sample, or two renders that must agree (source and min, or a repeat) and differ beyond the measured tolerance, in the first render of an item, fails the run. A clean re-render does not clear it. This corrects the earlier reasoning of this record and of T6 §16 ("a defect of the library reproduces on every render"): a defect that is intermittent is not disproved by a clean re-render. A player that loses a note once in a while loses it when it is not being re-rendered, too.

**Re-renders are diagnostics.** An item that fails its first attempt is rendered again, at most twice, stopping at the first clean one, and at most 6 items per case (`MAX_DIAGNOSED`; the budget limits the diagnostics only, never the verdict). Every attempt is counted exactly (`attempts`, `diagnosticRenderAttempts`, `diagnosticRenders`; the old fixed `renders: 2` is gone) and recorded in an observation, "first-attempt failures and their diagnostic re-renders (the verdict is the first attempt's; a clean re-render does not clear it)", with the first attempt's reasons, each re-render's result and the outcome:

| Outcome | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| `clean` | first attempt passed |
| first attempt failed; a re-render was clean | intermittent; the verdict stays failed |
| first attempt failed; reproduced in every re-render | deterministic |
| first attempt failed; not re-rendered | diagnostic budget spent |

A failing check's detail names the outcome (`[diagnostic: a re-render was clean]`). For source/min and repeat pairs each re-render also records which side moved (`newAvsFirstB`, `firstAvsNewB`). In `render.js`, measurements, WAV files and the analysis checks come from first attempts only, and a diagnostic re-render with a NaN or an infinity fails its own check ("diagnostic re-renders finite").

**What stayed.** `sameEngineSample` (Chromium 5e-4, WebKit 1e-6, Firefox 0) is the measured floating-point summation tolerance and is applied to the first attempt: `compareRenders` reports `identical`, `summation` (within it) or `mismatch` (a failure). No tolerance value changed; `tolerances.js` only says in its comments that nothing there is an allowance for a glitch. The 7 dB cross-engine allowance is untouched and unrelated.

**New finiteness checks.** `short-notes` had none, and its `maxDiff` let a later sample overwrite a NaN. Every render now counts its non-finite samples in the page (`nf`), and the held, completed, release-level, zero-length and preroll cases each fail on any.

**Code.** `tests/browser/lib/first-attempt.js` is pure: `judgeHeld`, `judgeCompleted`, `compareRenders`, `attempts`, `comparePair`, `createLog`, the fault injector. The specs call it. `tests/node/first-attempt.test.cjs` checks it, and drives `short-notes` on a scripted page.

## 3. Regression cases: a bad first result followed by a clean retry still fails

Fault injection into the first attempt only; every re-render is clean. The environment variable `TINYSYNTH_INJECT_FIRST_ATTEMPT_FAULT=<site>:<kind>` (sites `held`, `completed` in `short-notes`; `render`, `repeat` in `render`; kinds `transient` = a 0.5 step over 128 samples, `quiet` = the note's samples zeroed, `nonfinite` = a NaN sample) corrupts the first attempt of the first item of each case, once. It is unset in every normal run.

**Node (`tests/node/first-attempt.test.cjs`, 19 tests, in `npm test`).** The pure judges and `comparePair` on synthetic renders for all three kinds and the source/min mismatch; the `short-notes` held and completed cases on a scripted page whose first call is bad and whose re-render is clean: the run fails, `intermittent` is 1, and the counters are exact (held: 257 page calls for 256 first attempts and one diagnostic; completed: 2 diagnostic renders per failing program; a budget of 6 items with every failure still reported).

## 4. Investigation of the glitch

All probes are standalone (`_evidence/t13-1-flake/probe/probe.js` and `repro.js`): one note rendered `n` times, each in a fresh `OfflineAudioContext` and synth, each render hashed, the most common hash taken as the reference, every other render analysed. Terminating renders only (rule 10). WebKit 26.6 on linux-x64.

**Which renders.** In the probes the glitch hits the quality-0 noise programs 122 (Seashore), 126 (Applause) and 127 (Gunshot), all `n0` noise at a low `playbackRate` (0.05 to 0.15), and at quality 1 programs 126 and 127. Programs 40, 119 and (q1) 125 did not glitch there, though the spec itself saw q0 119, q1 120 and q1 125 and a drum, so it is not exclusive to three programs. The size reaches 0.38 of full scale on program 127 (0.72 at q1), audible by any measure.

**Rate.** Standalone, 44.1 kHz, one note per context, no stress, library `source`, q0 programs 122, 126 and 127 together: 24 glitches in 44,000 renders (1 in 1,800). `min`: 5 in 6,000. 48 kHz: 2 in 6,000. Quality 1 (programs 125 to 127): 4 in 10,000. Both builds and both sample rates are affected. GC stress (an allocation loop, or 4 ms busy bursts on the main thread during the render) did not raise it (1 in 4,000 renders for each, 2,400 of them noise renders, against 5 in 4,000 without). In the spec the rate per render is lower (about 9e-5, §5) because most programs are not noise.

**What the glitch is.** The glitched render is the reference delayed by exactly 128 frames (one render quantum; once 256) from some quantum on. Normalized cross-correlation of glitched against reference is 0.98 to 1.000 at lag -128 or -256 in 17 of the 23 glitches analysed; 4 were not evaluable (the compared window lay in silence), 1 correlated at lag -99 and 1 not at all (0.41). It begins at a quantum boundary of the compressor's input (the first differing sample is 8 + 128k or 64 + 128k frames in: the compressor's 264-frame pre-delay is 2 quanta + 8 frames) and lasts to the end of the sound. The noise buffer's content is identical in every render (hash). So the noise after some quantum lags by one quantum: one 128-frame block of the looped `AudioBufferSourceNode`'s output was dropped or repeated, in WebKit's render of the source or its inputs, and the library's data is unchanged.

**Ruled out as the cause** (q0 program 122, library `source`, the control rate there 3 in 8,000):

| Candidate | Evidence |
| --- | --- |
| the compressor | output wired straight to the destination: 4 in 12,000 |
| the LFO and detune | LFO disconnected: 4 in 8,000; `chmod` not connected to `detune`: 2 in 8,000 |
| the stereo panner | bypassed: 27 in 12,000 |
| the constructor's warm-up note | stopped and disconnected: 48 in 12,000 (higher, not lower) |
| the library's 60 ms scheduler | interval cleared: 12 in 6,000 (higher) |
| GC pressure | see Rate |
| sample rate, build | 44.1 and 48 kHz, `source` and `min` all affected |
| generated noise | identical buffer hash in every render |

Removing the warm-up note, the panner or the scheduler made it more frequent, not less; whether those removals matter (they change what the render thread does at quantum 0) was not pursued.

**Library-free reproduction: none, in about 104,000 renders.** A looping `n0`-sized noise buffer at rate 0.05 in an `OfflineAudioContext` of the same length: plain (4,000), with an LFO into `detune` (4,000 and 30,000), without the detune connection (4,000), with the library's output chain of gain, stereo panner, gain and compressor (32,000) and with that chain minus the compressor (30,000): 0 glitches. (The library-free renders take 3 to 5 ms against the library's 33 ms, which mostly builds a synth, so the main thread is much less busy in them. A 30,000-render run of the compressor chain crashed the WebKit page on memory and was repeated in four 8,000 runs.)

**Upstream.** `3d75aee`, the unmodified upstream build, with Math.random seeded before every construction and the context installed with `setAudioContext`, in bounded runs of 1,500 renders per program (upstream leaks contexts without `dispose()`, and a 12,000-render run was killed by WebKit's 16 GB limit): q0 program 122 1 in 1,500, 126 2 in 1,500, 127 0 in 1,500: 3 in 4,500, the fork's rate, and the one that could be analysed (126) shows the same -128 frame lag. So the glitch is inherited from upstream's graph, not introduced by the fork. (An earlier upstream run without the seeding differed on every render, because upstream's noise is unseeded, and is not evidence.)

**Conclusion.** A one-quantum slip in WebKit's render of looped, low-rate `AudioBufferSourceNode`s, reproducible with the library (both builds, and upstream) and not reproduced without it. Whether it is a WebKit defect or something in how the library builds that graph which WebKit mishandles is **not resolved**. Nothing here isolates a browser defect independently, so no check is quarantined.

**Unresolved:**
- the missing step between the library's graph and the library-free one (the main-thread work per render, the number of nodes per context, the library's note path);
- whether the slip is in the source node or in a downstream input;
- whether it also hits other sounds, at a lower rate (the spec saw q0 119, q1 120 and 125 and a drum);
- physical Safari: nothing was run there.

**Recommended follow-up (root cause, to be filed).** Bisect from the library toward a minimal page on WebKit. Start with the library's exact note path (the `n0` buffer from `setAudioContext`, `loop`, `playbackRate` 0.05 to 0.15, `start(t)` at 0.05 s, the operator and channel gain chain) in a page of its own, and add one library element at a time (the 16-channel graph, the warm-up note, `setTargetAtTime` on the gains, a synth built per render, the seeded buffer fill through `getChannelData`) until the rate reaches 1 in 1,800; run the same page in a newer WebKit build. If WebKit's, file it with that page; if a library pattern triggers it, change the pattern in the engine (a separate library change), and either way add a stress gate in CI that measures the first-attempt rate. The seeded noise path is worth reading first, because the buffer is filled by writing into `getChannelData(0)` after `createBuffer` and WebKit may share or copy that storage; upstream has the same fill, with `Math.random`.

## 5. First-attempt failure rates (how often CI will go red)

Natural first-attempt events (not injected), WebKit, with the spec's checks:

| Runs | Spec | Natural first-attempt events |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 12 (`base-*`) | short-notes, old spec | 3 (1 held failure; 2 completed, then absorbed by the retry) |
| 14 (`fix-*`) | short-notes, #65 spec | 2 (held, absorbed by the retry) |
| 5 (fault runs) | short-notes | 2 (held min q0 122; completed min q1 120) |
| 4 (fault runs) | render | 1 (gm-drums repeat, q0 48 kHz) |
| 1 (`final-3engines`) | render and short-notes, the new spec, all three engines | 0 |

- **WebKit short-notes:** 7 events in 31 runs, about 1 run in 4 to 5 (23 %, with a wide interval: 10 % to 41 % at 95 %), at about 2,560 renders per run, about 9e-5 per render. Each would now be a red run.
- **WebKit render:** 1 event in 4 runs here, and T6's record of 2 differing parts in about 13 runs (T6 §16): roughly 1 run in 8, from low counts.
- **Chromium and Firefox:** 0 in the one full run here, and none recorded in T6, T13 or the matrix history. Chromium's summation-order differences stay inside its 5e-4 tolerance.
- **CI.** The browser matrix runs 3 shards per engine. Expect a red WebKit shard in about one PR run in three to four while the glitch exists. The diagnostics observation in the job's results says whether a re-render was clean.
